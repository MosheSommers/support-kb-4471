How to tell them apart

  • parser: the tenant writes the day first, and the report came in by app, email, or a booking-v2 form. Day and month swap: 7 April becomes 4 July.
  • hint: the tenant runs booking-v3 and the report came through the form. The form tells people to type MM/DD/YYYY, then reads what they typed day first. Day and month swap too, so it looks like the parser bug. The fix is in the form, not the parser.
  • one-day: the date moves by one day, and day and month stay put. Tuesday becomes Monday; Lisbon and Toronto see different days for the same meeting. That points at time zones, not at date order. The desk had marked these in its notes but filed them under the parser.

Why they were merged

Under decision 0001 we merge on subject alone. All three problems produce the same subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”), so the subject rule cannot tell them apart. The intake tag can’t either: it guesses from the reporter’s browser, while the problem depends on the tenant and the way the report came in.

Where they stand

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October:

  • parser: fixed (fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales).
  • hint: won’t fix. The booking-v3 form is retired, and its hint went with it.
  • one-day: not covered by either. It stays open. When a report says the date moved by one day, check the time zones of everyone on the invite, and add it to the one-day ticket.
